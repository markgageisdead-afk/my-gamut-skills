# San Antonio Hospitality Experience Hunter

## Purpose

Identify public opportunities with San Antonio restaurants, cocktail bars, boutique hotels, retailers, and nightlife venues that may benefit from a recurring photo-booth residency, seasonal activation, or branded guest experience. Focus on Southtown, the River Walk, the Pearl, downtown, and other active hospitality corridors.

This skill does not send messages, bypass access controls, or collect private personal information. It uses publicly available business information and public business contact paths.

## Operating procedure

### 1. Define the search window and partnership type

Ask for or infer:

- Target date range
- Preferred San Antonio neighborhood or corridor
- Venue type: restaurant, bar, hotel, retailer, or mixed-use property
- Preferred arrangement: one-time activation, monthly residency, seasonal placement, or long-term lease
- Desired activation: photo booth, branded content station, guest photo moment, or lead-capture experience

Default to opportunities in the next 90 days and recurring placements that could support mid-week traffic or weekend programming.

### 2. Discover hospitality signals

Search publicly available sources such as:

- official venue websites and event calendars
- public Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, and other social pages visible without login
- Yelp, Google Business listings, local hospitality publications, and neighborhood guides
- San Antonio Current, Visit San Antonio, Pearl, River Walk, and local tourism calendars
- public opening announcements, menu launches, hotel programming, and retail event pages

Search for signals including:

- new restaurant, bar, hotel, or retail opening
- grand opening San Antonio
- new menu launch
- brunch, pool party, happy hour, girls’ night, or recurring nightlife programming
- aesthetic, photogenic, or guest-generated content
- seasonal programming and high-traffic weekends
- venue expansion, renovation, or refreshed branding

Do not treat a search snippet or social post as confirmed on its own. Open the source page and record the source URL and date checked.

### 3. Extract and normalize

For each candidate, capture:

- venue name and type
- neighborhood, street, or location
- public opening, event, or programming date
- official website and social URLs
- public business contact or contact-page URL, if published
- recurring programming and likely high-traffic periods
- evidence of user-generated content or guest photo sharing
- whether a dedicated photo-op or photo booth is publicly visible
- notes about brand style, audience, and operational fit

Use ISO dates (`YYYY-MM-DD`) when possible. Preserve the original date text when ambiguous. Deduplicate venues by normalized name and location.

### 4. Qualify partnership opportunities

Apply these tags:

- `[HIGH PRIORITY]` — active venue with public evidence of guest-generated content, recurring traffic, and no dedicated photo activation found in checked sources
- `[RECURRING FIT]` — brunches, nightlife, pool events, or seasonal programming could support a repeat placement
- `[MID-WEEK TRAFFIC FIT]` — venue appears suited to a Thursday or other slower-period promotion
- `[BRANDED CONTENT FIT]` — guests already share venue content and a branded photo experience could extend that behavior
- `[HOTEL / PREMIUM FIT]` — boutique hotel, luxury hospitality, or premium guest experience
- `[NEEDS VERIFICATION]` — venue status, programming, ownership, or contact details are incomplete or conflicting
- `[LOW CONFIDENCE]` — only a listing snippet or unverified social post was found

Suggested score:

- +3 recurring programming or clear repeat-traffic opportunity
- +3 strong public evidence of guest-generated content
- +2 new opening, menu launch, renovation, or refreshed brand
- +2 premium audience or visually distinctive venue
- +2 no photo activation found in checked sources
- +1 venue is in a target hospitality corridor
- −2 public business contact unavailable
- −2 venue status or event details unverified

Interpretation: 7+ is high priority; 4–6 is a research/outreach candidate; 0–3 is monitor.

Never claim that a venue has no photo opportunity or activation unless the checked sources support that conclusion. Use “no dedicated activation found in checked sources” when appropriate.

### 5. Recommend the partnership model

Select an evidence-based model:

- mid-week traffic booster
- monthly or quarterly residency
- seasonal brunch or pool-party activation
- branded guest-content station
- revenue-share pilot
- fixed monthly placement or lease discussion

Do not promise guaranteed traffic, social reach, revenue, or conversion. Describe the model as a proposal for discussion, subject to venue approval, operating requirements, insurance, space, power, staffing, and commercial terms.

### 6. Draft, do not send, outreach

Use the public venue name and confirmed details only. Keep the message concise and specific.

Example:

> Hey [Venue Name] team — your space in [Neighborhood] has a strong visual identity, and I noticed guests are already sharing content from [specific public observation]. We provide compact branded photo activations that can support a Thursday promotion, brunch, or seasonal residency while giving guests an easy way to share the venue. Would you be open to reviewing a simple monthly pilot for [specific programming period]?

If a revenue-share or lease model is suggested, present it as an option rather than a commitment. Do not contact private individuals through personal accounts when a public business channel is unavailable.

### 7. Output format

Return:

1. A short summary of the search window, geography, sources, and limitations.
2. A table of all discovered venues with type, location, public signals, programming, source URLs, tags, and score.
3. A ranked shortlist with the recommended partnership model and evidence.
4. Draft outreach messages for the top qualified venues only.
5. A follow-up checklist covering venue permission, placement, power, insurance, staffing, data-consent language, maintenance, and commercial terms.

Always include source URLs and the checked-on date for every lead.

## Safety and quality rules

- Use only publicly available information and respect site terms, robots directives, rate limits, and platform access controls.
- Do not scrape behind logins, evade bot protections, or collect sensitive personal data.
- Treat email addresses and phone numbers as business contact data only when the source presents them for business contact.
- Distinguish confirmed facts, reasonable inferences, and unknowns.
- Never fabricate a venue, opening date, event, owner, contact, audience, or social-performance claim.
- If web access is unavailable, provide a research plan and clearly label results as incomplete.
- Any guest email or SMS capture must be voluntary, clearly disclosed, and handled according to the approved activation provider’s privacy and consent practices.
