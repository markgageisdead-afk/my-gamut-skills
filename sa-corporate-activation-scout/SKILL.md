# San Antonio Corporate Activation Scout

## Purpose

Identify business opportunities in Bexar County where a sponsor-branded photo booth, event activation, or branded guest experience could fit a company launch, expansion, anniversary, or premium property event. Focus on public, verifiable trigger events in San Antonio neighborhoods such as downtown, the Medical Center, Stone Oak, and the Rim.

This skill does not send messages, bypass access controls, or collect private personal information. It uses only public business information and public contact details published by organizations.

## Operating procedure

### 1. Define the search window

Ask for or infer:

- Target date range
- Preferred geography in San Antonio
- Minimum company size or event scale
- Activation type: photo booth, branded guest experience, welcome-back activation, lead capture booth, or premium event experience

Default to events occurring in the next 90 days within Bexar County and adjacent premium business corridors.

### 2. Discover triggers

Search publicly available sources such as:

- San Antonio Business Journal
- local business announcement pages
- city permit and inspection records when publicly accessible
- local chamber or neighborhood business pages
- corporate LinkedIn pages and company event pages that are visible without login
- luxury real-estate broker pages, brokerage openings, and broker preview announcements

Prioritize triggers including:

- grand opening
- ribbon cutting
- new office
- office expansion
- relocation
- anniversary milestone
- open house
- luxury broker preview
- leasing office opening
- new showroom or studio launch

Look for target industries:

- technology and SaaS startups
- healthcare and biotech offices
- high-end real-estate brokerages
- luxury residential and leasing operators
- hospitality groups and premium hospitality venues
- multi-family residential communities opening new leasing centers

### 3. Extract and normalize

For each candidate, capture:

- company name
- trigger event or announcement type
- date and time
- location and neighborhood
- event URL
- source URL and checked-on date
- primary business category or industry
- known organizer or host company
- public business contact details if published
- notes on event size, audience, or branding signal

Use ISO dates (`YYYY-MM-DD`) whenever the source provides enough information. Preserve original wording when dates are ambiguous.

Do not treat search snippets as facts. Open the source page and confirm the details before adding a lead.

### 4. Qualify leads

A lead is relevant if there is public evidence of:

- a business milestone or opening event
- a premium audience or professional guest list
- a clear marketing or HR audience need
- a reasonable fit for a branded photo booth or experiential activation

Apply these tags:

- `[HIGH PRIORITY]` — new office, grand opening, or premium event with a clear business audience and no competing photo activation visible
- `[EXPANSION SIGNAL]` — company is growing, moving, or opening additional local presence
- `[HR / CULTURE FIT]` — internal staff or onboarding celebration angle is likely
- `[REAL ESTATE FIT]` — luxury property or leasing office opening with strong branded event potential
- `[B2B LEAD CAPTURE FIT]` — event likely attracts professional attendees who could share contact details
- `[NEEDS VERIFICATION]` — date, organizer, or venue details are incomplete or conflicting
- `[LOW CONFIDENCE]` — only a snippet or unverified social post was found

Suggested score:

- +3 public grand opening / ribbon cutting / expansion event
- +2 target industry match (tech, real-estate, hospitality, life sciences, professional services)
- +2 premium audience or high-end brand signal
- +2 strong event branding or guest-activation opportunity
- +1 public attendance or guest category suggests business-professional audience
- +1 event is within 90 days
- −2 contact or organizer unavailable
- −2 date or venue unverified

Interpretation: 7+ = high priority; 4–6 = research/outreach candidate; 0–3 = monitor.

### 5. Find the right decision-maker

Use public business information only to identify likely contacts such as:

- Marketing Director
- HR Manager
- Event Coordinator
- Office Manager
- Community or Brand Marketing lead
- Owner or partner for boutique real-estate groups

Do not infer or invent private details. If the decision-maker is not public, note the gap and keep the lead as a research candidate.

### 6. Build the activation angle

Recommend the most relevant positioning based on the event type.

For grand openings or office launches:

- internal morale and employee welcome-back activation
- premium brand moment for staff and partners
- light B2B data collection via photo-sharing opt-in

For luxury real-estate or leasing events:

- premium branded guest experience
- broker preview and VIP attraction
- lead capture for prospective clients or agent follow-up

For milestones and anniversaries:

- staff appreciation and community celebration
- sponsorship-friendly branded activation with strong photo-sharing

Keep claims proportionate to what is public and verifiable. Do not promise guaranteed attendance, leads, or sponsor ROI unless those terms are clearly approved.

### 7. Draft, do not send, outreach

Use only public naming and confirmed event details. Keep outreach professional, concise, and non-intrusive.

Example:

> Hi [Marketing/HR Director Name], congratulations on the upcoming opening of your new space in [Neighborhood]. For an event like this, we provide premium photo activations that help teams celebrate, attract attention, and capture guest data in a polished way. We’d love to explore a setup for [Date]. If this is relevant, I’d be happy to send a quick concept for your review.

If appropriate, add a specific source observation such as:

- “I noticed your company is opening a new office in The Rim.”
- “Your team is hosting a luxury broker preview this month.”
- “Your organization is celebrating a milestone anniversary and likely has a professional audience.”

### 8. Output format

Return:

1. A short summary of the search window, geography, sources, and limitations.
2. A table of discovered leads with company name, event type, date, venue, source, tags, score, and notes.
3. A ranked shortlist of the best opportunities with the recommended activation angle.
4. Draft outreach messages for the top leads only.
5. A verification checklist explaining what still must be confirmed before contact.

Always include source URLs and the checked-on date for every lead.

## Safety and quality rules

- Use only publicly available information and respect site terms, robots directives, rate limits, and platform access controls.
- Do not scrape behind logins, evade bot protections, or collect personal data.
- Do not impersonate staff, fake a relationship, or contact private individuals without a public business path.
- Distinguish confirmed facts, reasonable inferences, and unknowns.
- Never fabricate a company, event date, venue, organizer, or contact name.
- If web access is unavailable, provide a research plan and clearly label the results as incomplete.
- Keep all outreach factual, respectful, and aligned with the company’s public business context.
